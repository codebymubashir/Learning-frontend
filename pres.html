Recap: Classical Computer

  • Bit: the smallest unit of data. It is always 0 or 1.
  • Transistor: a tiny electric switch. ON = 1, OFF = 0.
  • Logic gates (AND, OR, NOT) combine bits to do calculations.
  • CPU = billions of transistors running gates very fast.
Analogy: a classical bit is like a light switch. It is either ON or OFF, nothing in between.
Roman Urdu: Normal computer bits use karta hai jo ya 0 hoti hai ya 1, bilkul light switch ki tarah.

Why Quantum Computers?

Factoring

Breaking big numbers into primes. Classical computers take extremely long. Shor's algorithm can do it much faster. (This is why RSA encryption is at risk.)

Simulating nature

Molecules and materials follow quantum rules. Quantum computers can simulate them for new medicines and batteries.

Searching

Grover's algorithm searches unsorted data in about √N steps instead of N.
Quantum computers are not "faster at everything". They are better at specific problems.

What is a Qubit?

  • A qubit (quantum bit) is the basic unit of quantum information.
  • Its two basic states are written |0〉 and |1〉.
  • Unlike a bit, it can be in a mix of both at the same time.
Analogy: a coin lying on a table is heads or tails (classical bit). A coin spinning in the air is a mix of both until it lands (qubit).
Roman Urdu: Qubit ek ghoomte hue sikke ki tarah hai. Jab tak girta nahi, woh head aur tail dono ka mix hota hai.

Superposition

|ψ〉 = α|0〉 + β|1〉

  • α and β tell us how much of each state is in the qubit.
  • Chance of getting 0 = |α|², chance of getting 1 = |β|². They add up to 100%.
  • Measurement: when we look at the qubit, the spinning coin lands. We get only a normal 0 or 1 and the superposition is gone.
With n qubits, a quantum computer works with 2n states together. 10 qubits = 1024 states, 50 qubits = over a quadrillion.

Entanglement

  • Two qubits can be linked so they act as one system.
  • Measure one and you instantly know the result of the other, even if they are far apart.
  • Example: the Bell state (|00〉 + |11〉)/√2 gives either 00 or 11, never 01 or 10.
Analogy: a pair of gloves in two sealed boxes. Open one box and find the left glove, and you instantly know the other is the right glove.
Roman Urdu: Entanglement mein do qubits aapas mein jud jate hain. Ek ko dekho to doosre ka result pata chal jata hai.

Decoherence: The Biggest Enemy

  • Qubits are very fragile. Heat, noise, and vibration disturb them.
  • When that happens, the qubit loses its quantum state. This is called decoherence.
  • T1 = time before a qubit falls from |1〉 to |0〉. T2 = time before superposition information fades.
  • All gates must finish before the qubit decoheres.
Analogy: an ice cube in the sun. You must use it before it melts.

What is a Quantum Gate?

  • A gate is an operation that changes the state of qubits, like a logic gate for bits.
  • Gates are reversible (unitary): no information is thrown away, unlike AND / OR.
  • Mathematically, a gate is a matrix that multiplies the qubit's state.
  • Picture: a gate rotates the qubit's arrow on a sphere (the Bloch sphere).
Roman Urdu: Quantum gate qubit ki state ko ghumata hai. Gates ulte bhi ho sakte hain, isliye information zaya nahi hoti.

Two-Qubit Gate: CNOT

  • CNOT = Controlled-NOT. It has a control qubit and a target qubit.
  • Rule: if control = 1, flip the target. If control = 0, do nothing.

Example Circuit: Making Entanglement

Start with two qubits in |00〉

  • Step 2: apply CNOT (qubit 1 is control, qubit 2 is target).
  • Result: (|00〉 + |11〉)/√2, the Bell state. Measuring gives 00 or 11 with 50% chance each.

Quantum Control: Applying Gates

Gates on paper are simple. In hardware, we must physically control each qubit.

  • Superconducting: send precise microwave pulses (about 4 to 8 GHz). Pulse shape and length decide which gate happens.
  • Trapped ions: use carefully tuned laser pulses.
  • Calibration: every qubit is slightly different, so pulses must be tuned regularly.
Analogy: like pushing a child on a swing. You must push at exactly the right rhythm and strength.

Cooling and Readout

Cooling

Superconducting qubits sit in a dilution refrigerator at about 15 millikelvin, colder than outer space. This reduces heat noise and lets the circuits superconduct.

Readout

To measure a qubit, a weak microwave signal is sent to it. The reflected signal changes depending on |0〉 or |1〉. Electronics turn this into a classical 0 or 1.
The control electronics are classical (normal digital hardware). A quantum computer is always a hybrid of quantum + classical parts.

Quantum Error Correction

  • Qubits make mistakes often, and we cannot simply copy a qubit to back it up.
  • Solution: spread one piece of information across many physical qubits. Together they form one reliable logical qubit.
  • Extra helper qubits keep checking for errors without measuring the data itself. A popular method is the surface code.
  • Cost: one logical qubit may need hundreds to thousands of physical qubits.
Analogy: sending an important message through many friends and taking the majority answer.

The Architecture Stack

1. Application: the algorithm (Shor, Grover, chemistry simulation)
2. Compiler: converts circuits into the gates the chip supports and maps them to physical qubits
3. Error correction layer: logical qubits built from physical ones
4. Control hardware: pulse generators, readout electronics (classical)
5. Qubit chip: physical qubits inside the cooling system
Roman Urdu: Upar se neeche: algorithm, compiler, error correction, control electronics, aur sabse neeche asli qubits.

Challenges Today

  • Noise and decoherence: qubits are unstable.
  • Scaling: wiring and cooling thousands of qubits is very hard.
  • Connectivity: on many chips, a qubit can only talk to its neighbors.
  • NISQ era: today's machines are Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum: a few hundred to about a thousand noisy qubits, not yet fully error-corrected.

Summary

  • A qubit can be in superposition and can be entangled with others.
  • Gates (X, H, Z, CNOT) are reversible operations that transform qubit states.
  • Control means pulses, extreme cooling, and readout electronics.
  • Error correction is the key to building large, reliable machines.
  • The architecture is a stack: algorithm, compiler, control, qubits.
